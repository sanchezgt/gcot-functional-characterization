enunciado: A 2-kg block is released from rest on a frictionless incline that makes an angle of 30 degrees with the horizontal. What is the magnitude of the block's acceleration along the incline, in m/s^2? Use g = 9.8 m/s^2.
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I identify the forces on the block: its weight, mg, acting straight down, and the normal force from the incline, perpendicular to the surface. There is no friction.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Next, I resolve the weight along the incline. The component of the weight parallel to the surface is mg sin(theta), and the normal force has no component along the surface, so the net force along the incline is mg sin(theta).

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Newton's second law along the incline gives ma = mg sin(theta), so a = g sin(theta). With theta = 30 degrees and sin(30°) = 0.5, a = 9.8 * 0.5.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me verify a = g sin(theta) with two limiting cases. At theta = 90 degrees the incline is vertical, sin(90°) = 1, and a = g, which is free fall, as it should be. At theta = 0 the surface is flat, sin(0) = 0, and a = 0, so a block at rest stays at rest. Both limits check out, so the formula holds.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
So a = 4.9 m/s^2.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The block accelerates down the incline at 4.9 m/s^2.

Final answer: 4.9.
