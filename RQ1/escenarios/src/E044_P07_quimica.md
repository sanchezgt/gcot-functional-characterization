enunciado: What is the pH of a 0.001 M aqueous solution of hydrochloric acid at 25 °C?
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We want the pH of 0.001 M HCl. pH is defined as -log10 of the hydrogen ion concentration, so the task is to find [H+].

## PROGRESSION:ABANDONED
A first approach is to treat the dissociation as an equilibrium: HCl has a very large Ka, and with an ICE table, x for the dissociated amount, the condition is Ka = x^2 / (0.001 - x), a quadratic to solve for x.

## ALTERNATIVE:ADOPTED | explicita
Alternatively, with a Ka that large the dissociation is essentially complete, so [H+] simply equals the nominal concentration, 0.001 M, and there is nothing to solve. This is much simpler, so I will use it and set the ICE table aside.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
With [H+] = 0.001 M = 10^-3 M, pH = -log10(10^-3) = 3.

## SUPPORT
Strong acids such as HCl, HBr, and HNO3 transfer their proton to water essentially completely, which is what makes them strong.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The pH of the solution is 3.

Answer: 3.
