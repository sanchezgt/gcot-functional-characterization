enunciado: Police officers executing a valid warrant to search a home for stolen televisions see a bag of cocaine lying on a table in the living room. May they seize the cocaine? Options: A. Yes, under the plain view doctrine B. No, because the warrant does not list drugs C. No, unless they first obtain a second warrant D. Yes, but only with the homeowner's consent
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Officers executing a warrant for stolen televisions see a bag of cocaine on a table. We must decide whether they may seize it.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | implicita
One reading limits the officers to the warrant's list: televisions are named and cocaine is not, so the bag would have to stay where it is. Seizures during a lawful search, however, are not confined to the listed items when other evidence is in plain view, so that reading does not hold.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Under the plain view doctrine, officers may seize an item without a warrant if they are lawfully in the place from which they see it, its incriminating character is immediately apparent, and they have a lawful right of access to it.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The officers are lawfully in the living room under a valid warrant, and a room where televisions could be found is within the scope of the search, so they are where they are entitled to be when they see the bag; a bag of cocaine is evidently contraband.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | implicita
The incriminating character must be apparent without any further search, and the bag was lying in the open and was recognized on sight, with nothing moved or opened to identify it, so that requirement is met and the seizure stands.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The officers may seize the cocaine under the plain view doctrine, option A.

Answer: A.
