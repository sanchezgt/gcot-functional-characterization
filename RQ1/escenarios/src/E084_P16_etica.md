enunciado: During a respiratory epidemic, a hospital that has activated crisis standards of care has one ventilator left. Two patients need it: an 85-year-old man with multiple organ failure and a very poor prognosis, who arrived first, and a 40-year-old woman with an excellent expected chance of recovery, who arrived second. How should the ventilator be allocated? Options: A. To the patient who arrived first B. By lottery between the two patients C. To the patient most likely to survive with treatment D. To the older patient, because he has the greater need
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I lay out the situation: one ventilator, two patients, and crisis standards of care in effect. The 85-year-old with multiple organ failure arrived first; the 40-year-old with an excellent expected chance of recovery arrived second.

## PROGRESSION:ABANDONED
My first approach is to weigh the four classic principles one at a time for each patient: autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, and justice. Starting with autonomy, both patients would want the ventilator, so that principle does not separate them.

## ALTERNATIVE:ADOPTED | implicita
Crisis standards of care, which this hospital has activated, already settle the question with a single criterion, the likelihood of benefit assessed with clinical scores of short-term survival, so the allocation is made on that basis.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
On that basis, the 40-year-old woman with an excellent expected chance of recovery has a far greater likelihood of surviving with ventilation than the patient with multiple organ failure, so the ventilator goes to her, and the other patient receives full palliative care.

## SUPPORT
The criterion aims to save the most lives with the resources available, and it is applied by a triage officer who is not part of the patients' treating team.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The ventilator should go to the patient most likely to survive with treatment, option C.

Final answer: C.
