## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I set up the picture: a 5 m ladder leans against a wall with its foot 3 m from the base of the wall, and I want the height at which its top touches the wall.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Next, I see that the ladder, the ground, and the wall form a triangle. Treating the corner where the wall meets the ground as a right angle, the ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height h satisfies 3^2 + h^2 = 5^2.

## VERIFICATION:INCONCLUSIVE | implicita
That right angle holds only if the wall stands vertical on level ground, and the problem states neither. A leaning wall or sloping ground would change the angle at the corner, and nothing in the data fixes it one way or the other.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Taking the wall as vertical and the ground as level, h^2 = 25 - 9 = 16, so h = 4 m.

## SUPPORT
The Pythagorean theorem relates the sides of a right triangle: the square of the side opposite the right angle, here the ladder, equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The top of the ladder reaches 4 m up the wall.

Final answer: 4.
