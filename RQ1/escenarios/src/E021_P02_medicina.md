She has no fever, nausea, or flank pain. Her temperature is 37.1°C. There is mild suprapubic tenderness and no costovertebral angle tenderness. Urinalysis is positive for nitrites and leukocyte esterase. She is not pregnant. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment? Options: A. Oral nitrofurantoin for 5 days B. Intravenous ciprofloxacin C. Intramuscular ceftriaxone followed by hospital admission D. Observation without antibiotics
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I summarize the case: a young, healthy, nonpregnant woman with three days of dysuria, frequency, and suprapubic discomfort, afebrile, with suprapubic tenderness and a urinalysis positive for nitrites and leukocyte esterase.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Dysuria and frequency with nitrites and leukocyte esterase in the urine mean a bacterial urinary tract infection. The next question is where the infection is, because that determines the treatment.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The symptoms are limited to the bladder: burning, frequency, and suprapubic discomfort, with no flank pain and no costovertebral angle tenderness. That is acute uncomplicated cystitis.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | implicita
An infection that had spread beyond the bladder would show systemic signs, above all fever and often nausea. Her temperature is 37.1°C and she has no nausea, so the diagnosis of uncomplicated cystitis holds.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Uncomplicated cystitis in a healthy nonpregnant woman is treated as an outpatient with a short course of an oral agent. Nitrofurantoin for 5 days is a first-line choice.

## SUPPORT
Nitrofurantoin concentrates in the urine and reaches high levels in the bladder, which is why it works well for an infection confined there.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most appropriate treatment is oral nitrofurantoin for 5 days, option A.

Final answer: A.
