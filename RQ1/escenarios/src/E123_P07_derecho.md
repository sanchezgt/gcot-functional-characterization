enunciado: In a prosecution for bank robbery, the prosecutor offers a letter that the defendant wrote from jail to his sister, which says, "I hid the money in the garage." The defendant does not testify, and his lawyer objects that the letter is hearsay. Under the Federal Rules of Evidence, how should the court rule? Options: A. Sustain the objection, because the letter is hearsay not within any exception B. Overrule the objection, because the letter is a statement of an opposing party C. Sustain the objection unless the prosecutor shows that the defendant is unavailable and the letter was a statement against interest D. Sustain the objection, because private letters between family members are privileged
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Need the ruling on a hearsay objection to the defendant's own letter, "I hid the money in the garage," offered by the prosecution. Out-of-court statement, offered for its truth.

## PROGRESSION:ABANDONED
Route: statement against interest, FRE 804(b)(3). Saying he hid the robbery money exposes him to criminal liability, so a reasonable person would not say it unless it were true; that element is met. Next element: unavailability. He does not testify; whether that makes him unavailable turns on whether he invokes the privilege against self-incrimination, so the next step is the record on that point.

## ALTERNATIVE:ADOPTED | explicita
Alternatively: the prosecution is offering the defendant's own words against him. Under FRE 801(d)(2)(A), a party's own statement offered by the opposing party is not hearsay at all, so no exception and no unavailability are needed. Shorter route; switching to it and dropping the 804(b)(3) analysis.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Elements: a statement made by a party, offered against that party. The defendant wrote the letter; the prosecution, his opponent, offers it against him. Not hearsay. Objection overruled.

## SUPPORT
The rule asks for no showing that the statement was reliable or against interest when made: a party cannot object that he had no chance to cross-examine himself.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Overrule; opposing party's statement. Answer: B.
