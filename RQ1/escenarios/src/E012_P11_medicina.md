Which of the following is the most appropriate initial diagnostic test? Options: A. Serum TSH B. Plasma free metanephrines C. 24-hour urinary free cortisol D. Renal artery Doppler ultrasonography
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Okay, let's see. A young woman with episodes of headache, sweating, and palpitations lasting about 20 minutes, with blood pressure spiking to 196/112 during an episode and normal in between. Thyroid tests are normal. The question asks for the best initial diagnostic test.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
Alternatively, could this be hyperthyroidism? Palpitations and sweating fit it. But hyperthyroidism causes sustained symptoms rather than discrete 20-minute attacks, and her thyroid function tests are already normal, which rules it out directly. So I'll drop that possibility.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The triad of episodic headache, sweating, and palpitations with paroxysmal hypertension is the classic presentation of pheochromocytoma, a catecholamine-secreting tumor of the adrenal medulla. The attacks correspond to bursts of catecholamine release.

## SUPPORT
The normal blood pressure between episodes is typical, since many of these tumors secrete intermittently.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The initial diagnostic test for a suspected pheochromocytoma is a biochemical one that demonstrates excess catecholamine production, and the test of choice is plasma free metanephrines, the most sensitive screening test for this tumor.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me double-check the choice of test. Metanephrines are the metabolites of epinephrine and norepinephrine, they are produced continuously by the tumor even between attacks, and plasma free metanephrines have the highest sensitivity among the initial tests. So the choice holds.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the answer should be B, plasma free metanephrines.
