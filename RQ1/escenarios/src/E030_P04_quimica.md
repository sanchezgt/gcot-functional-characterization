enunciado: Propane burns completely in oxygen according to the unbalanced equation C3H8 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O. How many moles of O2 are required to burn one mole of propane completely?
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I write down what has to be balanced: C3H8 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O, and I want the coefficient of O2 for one mole of propane.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
I start with carbon and hydrogen, which appear in only one product each. Three carbons in propane give 3 CO2, and eight hydrogens give 4 H2O. So far: C3H8 + O2 -> 3 CO2 + 4 H2O.

## ERROR
Now the oxygen. The products contain 3 x 2 = 6 oxygen atoms in the CO2 and 4 in the water, 10 oxygen atoms in total, so I need 10 O2 on the left.

## VERIFICATION:REFUTED | implicita
Ten molecules of O2 would supply 20 oxygen atoms, twice the 10 atoms that the products contain.

## CORRECTION:RECOVERED | implicita
Each O2 carries two oxygen atoms, so 10 oxygen atoms come from 10 / 2 = 5 molecules of O2.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The balanced equation is C3H8 + 5 O2 -> 3 CO2 + 4 H2O.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So one mole of propane requires 5 moles of O2.

Final answer: 5.
