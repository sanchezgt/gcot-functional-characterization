enunciado: A nurse notices that a colleague administered a patient's medication at twice the prescribed dose. The patient suffered no harm. The colleague asks the nurse not to mention the error to anyone. Which of the following is the most appropriate action for the nurse? Options: A. Report the error through the institution's incident reporting system B. Keep the matter private because no harm occurred C. Inform the patient's family directly D. Report the error only if the colleague makes a second error
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The situation: a colleague gave a double dose, the patient was not harmed, and the colleague asks for silence. We must choose the most appropriate action for the nurse who noticed the error.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
One alternative is to keep the matter between the two nurses, on the ground that no harm resulted and a report could damage a colleague. But incident reporting systems exist precisely to capture near misses before they cause harm, and concealing one defeats that purpose; loyalty to a colleague does not override the duty to patient safety. This option is rejected.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Professional codes require nurses to report medication errors through the institution's reporting system, regardless of whether harm occurred. The purpose is systemic: to identify why the error happened and to prevent recurrence.

## SUPPORT
Most institutions run these systems on a non-punitive basis: a report starts an analysis of the process that allowed a double dose to reach the patient.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The report should state what happened, the dose given, and the patient's condition, and the nurse can tell the colleague that she is filing it.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most appropriate action is to report the error through the incident reporting system, option A.

Answer: A.
