enunciado: A restaurant contracts with a supplier to buy and install a commercial oven for $20,000, of which $19,000 is the price of the oven and $1,000 is the charge for installation. The oven fails within a week of delivery. Which body of law governs the contract? Options: A. Article 2 of the UCC, because the predominant purpose of the contract is the sale of goods B. The common law of contracts, because the contract includes services C. Both, applied separately to the oven and to the installation D. Neither, because mixed contracts are governed by trade usage
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given a contract for the purchase and installation of a commercial oven for $20,000, of which $19,000 is the oven and $1,000 the installation, and an oven that fails within a week. We need the body of law that governs the contract.

## PROGRESSION:ABANDONED
I begin with the wording of the contract, to see whether it is framed as a sale or as a service: it speaks of buying and installing a commercial oven, which names both, so the wording alone points in two directions.

## ALTERNATIVE:ADOPTED | implicita
For a contract that mixes goods and services, courts ask which element predominates, and here the oven accounts for $19,000 of the $20,000 price, with the installation incidental to the purchase; that places the whole contract under Article 2 of the UCC, and the analysis proceeds under its rules.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Under Article 2, a merchant seller gives an implied warranty of merchantability, and an oven that fails within a week is not fit for its ordinary purpose, which gives the restaurant warranty remedies against the supplier.

## SUPPORT
The predominant purpose test asks what the parties mainly bargained for, and the share of the price that goes to the goods is one of the main factors courts weigh.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Thus final answer: A.
