enunciado: In a jurisdiction where adverse possession requires open, notorious, exclusive, continuous, and hostile possession for 10 years, a landowner fenced and used a strip of her neighbor's land for 12 years. For the first 5 years she did so under a written license from the neighbor; the neighbor then revoked the license in writing, and she continued to fence and use the strip for the remaining 7 years. Has she acquired title to the strip by adverse possession? Options: A. Yes, because 12 years of possession exceeds the 10-year period B. No, because the 5 permissive years do not count and only 7 years of hostile possession have run C. Yes, because fencing the strip alone satisfies the requirement D. No, because adverse possession is never available against a neighbor
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Need to decide whether 12 years of use, the first 5 under a license, satisfies a 10-year adverse possession requirement.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
Alternatively, one could count from the first day of use, since 12 total years exceeds the 10 required. But possession under the owner's permission is not hostile, and hostility is an element of the claim, so years spent under the license cannot be counted. That approach is rejected.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Hostile possession began when the license was revoked. From that point she possessed without permission, which is what hostility means.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Hostile years: 7, from revocation to the present. 7 is less than the 10 required. The claim fails on the duration element.

## SUPPORT
The other elements, open and notorious (the fence), exclusive, and continuous, are satisfied for the whole period, but they cannot substitute for the missing years.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Check against each element one more time: open and notorious, yes; exclusive, yes; continuous, yes; hostile, only from year 6 onward; duration of hostile possession, 7 years, short of 10. The conclusion holds.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
No title by adverse possession. Answer: B.
