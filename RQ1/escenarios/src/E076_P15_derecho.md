enunciado: A shopper slips on a spilled liquid in a supermarket aisle and sues the store for negligence. The liquid had been on the floor for about 40 minutes, no employee had inspected the aisle during that time, and there is no evidence that an employee caused the spill. Which of the following must the shopper establish about the store's knowledge of the spill? Options: A. That the store had actual knowledge of the spill B. That the store knew or, through reasonable inspection, should have known of the spill C. Nothing, because the store is strictly liable for conditions on its premises D. That an employee caused the spill
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: a shopper slipped on a liquid that had been on the floor for about 40 minutes, with no inspection of the aisle in that time and no evidence that an employee caused the spill. We need the knowledge element of the shopper's negligence claim.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
A store owes its customers, as business invitees, a duty of reasonable care to keep the premises safe. For a hazard that the store did not create, liability requires notice: either the store knew of the hazard, or the hazard existed long enough that a reasonable inspection would have discovered it. Here the liquid lay on the floor for about 40 minutes with no inspection, which may give the store that second kind of notice.

## VERIFICATION:INCONCLUSIVE | implicita
Whether 40 minutes is in fact long enough for it depends on how often a reasonable store of this kind inspects its aisles, and the facts give neither the store's inspection routine nor how busy the aisle was; that question would be left to the jury.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Either way, the element the shopper must establish is the same: that the store knew of the spill or, through reasonable inspection, should have known of it.

## SUPPORT
The notice requirement keeps a store from being treated as an insurer of every condition that arises on its premises without its fault.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Thus final answer: B.
