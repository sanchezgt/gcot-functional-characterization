enunciado: A 45-year-old woman who speaks only Spanish is scheduled for an elective colonoscopy next week. At the consent discussion, her 12-year-old son, who is fluent in English and Spanish, offers to interpret, and she says she would be fine with that. The clinic has a telephone interpreter service available at any time. What should the physician do? Options: A. Hold the consent discussion through the telephone interpreter service B. Let the son interpret, because the patient agrees C. Obtain consent in English, speaking slowly and simply D. Cancel the procedure until a family member over 18 can attend
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The facts: the patient speaks only Spanish, the colonoscopy is elective, her 12-year-old son offers to interpret and she agrees, and the clinic has a telephone interpreter service. We must decide how the consent discussion should be held.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | implicita
One option is to let the son interpret: he speaks both languages, he is already in the room, and his mother accepts. A child, however, cannot be relied on to render medical risks and procedures accurately, and the discussion would put on him details of his mother's health that a 12-year-old should not have to carry, so that option is set aside.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES | id=plan
Informed consent is valid only if the patient understands the nature, risks, benefits, and alternatives of the procedure. For a patient who does not speak English, that understanding depends on a qualified interpreter, so the physician should hold the consent discussion through the clinic's telephone interpreter service.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The interpreter renders the physician's explanation and the patient's questions in full, and the patient signs the consent form once her questions have been answered.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | implicita | objetivo=plan
A professional interpreter would be hard to justify only if waiting for one delayed needed care. The procedure is elective and the telephone service is available at any time, so the plan costs her nothing in timeliness while securing her understanding, and the choice of the interpreter stands.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The physician should hold the consent discussion through the telephone interpreter service, option A.

Answer: A.
