## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: "A ball is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from the top of a cliff 20 m high. Ignoring air resistance and taking g = 10 m/s^2, how far from the base of the cliff does the ball land?"

We need the horizontal distance from the base of the cliff to the landing point.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The horizontal and vertical motions are independent. Vertically, the ball starts with zero vertical velocity and falls 20 m under gravity, so h = (1/2) g t^2 gives t^2 = 2h / g = 2 * 20 / 10 = 4, and the flight time is t = 2 s.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | implicita
After 2 s of falling the vertical speed is g t = 20 m/s; the vertical speed grows uniformly from 0 to 20 m/s, so its average is 10 m/s, and 10 m/s for 2 s is 20 m, the height of the cliff.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Horizontally there is no acceleration, so the distance covered is x = v0 t = 15 * 2 = 30 m.

## SUPPORT
The horizontal speed stays at 15 m/s for the whole flight because gravity acts only vertically and air resistance is ignored.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the ball lands 30 m from the base of the cliff.

Thus final answer: 30.
