## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The statement has the form "if P, then Q", where P is "the number is divisible by 6" and Q is "the number is divisible by 3". We must choose its contrapositive.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The contrapositive of "if P, then Q" is "if not Q, then not P": the two parts change places and both are negated.

## SUPPORT
The contrapositive is logically equivalent to the original conditional, which is why a proof by contraposition can establish the original statement by proving its contrapositive instead.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Now we apply this form to our statement, negating each part and putting the negation of Q in the "if" position.

## ERROR
This gives: if a number is not divisible by 6, then it is not divisible by 3. That is option C.

## CORRECTION:RECOVERED | explicita
Actually, I negated the two parts but left them in their original order, which produces the inverse, not the contrapositive. With the parts swapped, the contrapositive is: if a number is not divisible by 3, then it is not divisible by 6, which is option B.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The contrapositive of the statement is option B.

Answer: B.
