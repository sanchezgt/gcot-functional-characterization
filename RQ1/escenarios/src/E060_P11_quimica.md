enunciado: Ethanol (CH3CH2OH) and dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3) are structural isomers with the same molecular formula, C2H6O. Which of the two has the higher boiling point?
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are comparing the boiling points of two isomers, ethanol and dimethyl ether, which share the formula C2H6O and therefore the same molar mass.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
One alternative line of reasoning is that, since the two compounds have the same formula and the same molar mass, they should boil at about the same temperature. But boiling point is governed by the strength of the forces between molecules, not by mass alone, and isomers can differ greatly in those forces. So that line of reasoning is set aside.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Ethanol has an O-H group. The hydrogen bonded to oxygen can form hydrogen bonds with the oxygen atoms of neighboring ethanol molecules, which are among the strongest intermolecular attractions.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Dimethyl ether has its oxygen bonded to two carbons and no O-H group, so its molecules cannot hydrogen-bond to one another; they interact only through weaker dipole-dipole and dispersion forces.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to separate the molecules into the gas phase, so ethanol should have the higher boiling point.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me verify the prediction against the known values: ethanol boils at 78 °C and dimethyl ether at -24 °C, a difference of about 100 degrees in the direction predicted. Confirmed.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Ethanol has the higher boiling point.

Answer: Ethanol.
