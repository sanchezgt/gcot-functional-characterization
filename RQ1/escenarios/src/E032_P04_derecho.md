enunciado: A driver runs a red light and strikes a pedestrian who was crossing outside the crosswalk. The jurisdiction follows a pure comparative negligence rule. The jury finds the driver 70% at fault and the pedestrian 30% at fault, and assesses the pedestrian's damages at $100,000. How much does the pedestrian recover? Options: A. $0 B. $30,000 C. $70,000 D. $100,000
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The facts: the driver ran a red light and hit a pedestrian who was crossing outside the crosswalk. The jury allocated fault 70% to the driver and 30% to the pedestrian and found damages of $100,000. The jurisdiction applies pure comparative negligence.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Both parties were negligent, so the question is how the pedestrian's own negligence affects her recovery under the applicable rule.

## ERROR
Because the pedestrian was partly at fault, her own negligence bars the claim, and she recovers nothing.

## VERIFICATION:REFUTED | implicita
Barring all recovery for any fault on the plaintiff's part is the contributory negligence rule, and the facts state that this jurisdiction follows a different rule, pure comparative negligence.

## CORRECTION:RECOVERED | implicita
Her claim is therefore not barred: under pure comparative negligence her damages are reduced by her 30% share of fault instead.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Reduced award: $100,000 x (1 - 0.30) = $100,000 x 0.70 = $70,000.

## SUPPORT
The rule is "pure" because the plaintiff recovers in proportion to the defendant's fault whatever the size of her own share of the fault.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The pedestrian recovers $70,000, option C.

Answer: C.
