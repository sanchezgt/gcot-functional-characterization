## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: "A bond has a face value of $1,000 and pays a 5% annual coupon. The bond currently trades at $950. What is its current yield?"

We need the current yield, which is the annual coupon payment divided by the current market price.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The annual coupon is 5% of the face value: 0.05 * 1,000 = 50 dollars per year.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The market price is 950 dollars. So the current yield is 50 / 950.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me double-check the definition before computing, because the coupon rate and the current yield are easy to confuse. The coupon rate is the coupon divided by the face value, which is the 5% given. The current yield is the coupon divided by the market price, so the denominator must be 950, not 1,000. The setup is correct.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
50 / 950 = 0.05263..., which is 5.26% when rounded to two decimal places.

## SUPPORT
The current yield is above the coupon rate because the bond trades below par: the same 50-dollar coupon is earned on a smaller investment.
