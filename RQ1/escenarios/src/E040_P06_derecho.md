## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Okay, let's see. An uncle promises $1,000 to his 19-year-old nephew for his 21st birthday, asks nothing in return, and the nephew does nothing in reliance. The uncle refuses to pay. Is the promise enforceable?

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
A promise is enforceable as a contract only if it is supported by consideration: something of legal value bargained for and given in exchange. The uncle asked for nothing, so nothing was bargained for. His motive, pride in his nephew, is not consideration, and a promise made out of affection is a gift promise.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | implicita
Promissory estoppel could still make the promise binding without consideration, since it enforces promises on which the promisee reasonably relied to his detriment; the facts state that the nephew did nothing in reliance on the letter.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
So the promise is a gratuitous promise with no consideration, and the uncle is not bound by it.

## SUPPORT
Consideration requires a bargain: the promisor asks for a performance or a return promise, and the promisee gives it in exchange for the promise.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the answer should be B: the promise is not enforceable because it lacks consideration.
