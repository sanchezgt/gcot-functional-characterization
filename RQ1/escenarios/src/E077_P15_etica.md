She understands the methods and their risks. Which of the following is the most appropriate action? Options: A. Provide contraceptive counseling and keep the visit confidential B. Inform her parents before prescribing C. Refuse until a parent gives consent D. Refer the matter to a court
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I identify the issue: a 16-year-old asks for contraception and asks that her parents not be told. The physician has to decide whether to provide care confidentially.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
At 16, and understanding the methods and their risks, she can consent to contraception herself, so the physician may provide it without involving her parents.

## VERIFICATION:INCONCLUSIVE | implicita
Whether a 16-year-old may consent on her own is set by the law of the jurisdiction, and the vignette does not say which law applies; the rules on minors' consent to contraception are not identical everywhere, so this cannot be fully settled from these facts.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The ethical guidance of medical societies supports providing contraceptive care confidentially to an adolescent who understands it, while encouraging, not requiring, communication with a trusted adult.

## SUPPORT
Adolescents are much more likely to seek sexual health care when confidentiality is assured, and withholding contraception does not stop sexual activity; it raises the risk of an unintended pregnancy.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most appropriate action is to provide contraceptive counseling and keep the visit confidential, option A.

Final answer: A.
