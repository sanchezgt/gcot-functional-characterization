## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: a 31-year-old woman with 3 months of palpitations, heat intolerance, anxiety, and weight loss despite a good appetite, a pulse of 112/min, a diffusely enlarged nontender thyroid with a bruit, bilateral proptosis with lid retraction, and a fine tremor. TSH is undetectable and free T4 is high. "Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?"

The suppressed TSH with an elevated free T4 means thyrotoxicosis of thyroid origin. We need its cause.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | implicita
One candidate for this biochemical picture is subacute granulomatous thyroiditis, which also releases stored hormone and suppresses TSH. That disorder, however, follows a viral illness, makes the gland painful and exquisitely tender, and does not affect the orbits, whereas her gland is nontender and her eyes are proptotic, so it does not fit her.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
A diffusely enlarged, nontender gland with a bruit, together with ophthalmopathy, in a young woman with thyrotoxicosis is the classic presentation of Graves disease, an autoimmune disorder in which antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor and drive the whole gland to grow and overproduce hormone.

## SUPPORT
The bruit reflects the greatly increased blood flow through a gland that is diffusely stimulated, and the proptosis comes from the same autoimmune process acting on the orbital fat and extraocular muscles.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the most likely diagnosis is Graves disease, option A.

Thus final answer: A.
