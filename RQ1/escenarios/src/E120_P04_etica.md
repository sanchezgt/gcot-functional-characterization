enunciado: A 75-year-old man is unconscious after a severe stroke, and a decision must be made about placing a feeding tube. He has no advance directive. His wife, who is his surrogate, says that he told her many times he would never want to be kept alive by machines, but she herself wants everything done because she cannot bear to lose him. On what basis should the decision be made? Options: A. The patient's previously expressed wishes, as reported by his wife B. The wife's own preference, because she is the surrogate C. The physician's view of the patient's best medical interest D. A vote of the hospital ethics committee
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Okay, let's see. An unconscious stroke patient, no advance directive, a feeding tube decision. His wife is the surrogate. She reports that he often said he'd never want to be kept alive by machines, but she wants everything done.

## ERROR
She's the legal surrogate, so her preference decides, and the feeding tube goes in as she wants.

## VERIFICATION:REFUTED | implicita
A surrogate speaks for the patient, not for herself. The standard of substituted judgment asks what the patient would choose, and the wife reports that he said many times he would never want to be kept alive by machines, which runs against her own wish. Her preference can't be what decides.

## CORRECTION:RECOVERED | implicita
So the decision rests on his own expressed wishes, as she reports them, and not on her preference; her role is to convey them.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The best interest standard is used only when the patient's wishes are unknown, and here they're known. So the physician and the wife should plan care that follows his wishes, and the wife should get support in her grief.

## SUPPORT
Substituted judgment extends the patient's autonomy into the period when he can no longer speak for himself.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the answer should be A.
