Laboratory studies show a hemoglobin of 9.0 g/dL, a mean corpuscular volume of 70 fL, a low serum ferritin, and a high total iron-binding capacity. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment? Options: A. Oral ferrous sulfate B. Intramuscular vitamin B12 C. Transfusion of packed red blood cells D. Recombinant erythropoietin
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I take stock: a young woman with fatigue, exertional dyspnea, and heavy periods; hemoglobin 9.0, MCV 70, low ferritin, high iron-binding capacity; hemodynamically stable. I need the most appropriate treatment.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
A hemoglobin of 9.0 is anemia, and an MCV of 70 makes it microcytic.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me verify that classification before building on it. The normal MCV range is about 80 to 100 fL, and 70 is clearly below it, so microcytic is right.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Microcytic anemia with a low ferritin and a high total iron-binding capacity is iron deficiency anemia: the body's iron stores are depleted and the transport protein has free capacity. The heavy menstrual bleeding explains the chronic iron loss.

## SUPPORT
Each milliliter of blood carries about half a milligram of iron, so months of heavy periods can exhaust the iron stores of a young woman.

## ERROR
Given a hemoglobin of 9.0, the treatment is a transfusion of packed red blood cells to restore her oxygen-carrying capacity quickly.

## VERIFICATION:REFUTED | explicita
Wait, let me check the indication for transfusion. Transfusion is reserved for hemodynamic instability, active severe bleeding, or a hemoglobin below about 7 g/dL. She is stable with a hemoglobin of 9.0 and a chronic, correctable cause, so transfusion is not indicated here.

## CORRECTION:RECOVERED | explicita
Correcting that: the treatment for stable iron deficiency anemia is iron replacement, and oral ferrous sulfate is the first-line form.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most appropriate treatment is oral ferrous sulfate, option A.

Final answer: A.
