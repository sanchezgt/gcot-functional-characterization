enunciado: A math club sells raffle tickets at $3 each or, as an alternative, in books of 5 tickets for $12. Leo wants exactly 13 tickets. He buys as many books as he can without going over 13 tickets and pays for the rest one at a time, and the club treasurer checks every receipt against the price list. How much does Leo pay, in dollars?
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES | distractor
We need the amount Leo pays for exactly 13 tickets. A single ticket costs $3, and the alternative is a book of 5 tickets for $12. The problem fixes how he buys: as many books as fit within 13 tickets, and the rest one at a time.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Step 1: 13 ÷ 5 = 2 with remainder 3, so Leo buys 2 books, which hold 10 tickets, and 3 single tickets.

## SUPPORT | distractor
Division with remainder matches this buying rule: the quotient counts the full books and the remainder counts the tickets left over, which is why the treasurer can confirm the 13 tickets on the receipt at a glance.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Step 2: the 2 books cost 2 × 12 = $24, and the 3 single tickets cost 3 × 3 = $9.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES | distractor
Step 3: the total on the receipt that the treasurer checks is 24 + 9 = $33.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Leo pays $33.

Answer: 33.
