enunciado: A residential lease states that rent is due on the first day of each month and that late payment is grounds for termination. For six consecutive months the tenant paid between the 8th and the 12th, and the landlord accepted each payment without objection. In the seventh month the tenant paid on the 10th; the landlord refused the payment and served a notice of termination for late payment, without any prior warning. Under general common-law principles, which of the following is the most likely outcome? Options: A. The termination is valid because the lease expressly makes late payment grounds for termination B. The termination is invalid because the landlord's course of conduct waived strict compliance until the tenant was notified that timely payment would again be required C. The termination is valid because waiver cannot apply to a written lease D. The termination is invalid because a landlord may never terminate for late payment
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given a lease that makes rent due on the first and treats late payment as grounds for termination. For six months the landlord accepted payments made between the 8th and the 12th without objecting. In the seventh month the landlord refused the late payment and terminated without warning. The question asks which outcome is most likely under general common-law principles.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The relevant doctrine is waiver by course of conduct. When a party repeatedly accepts performance that departs from the strict terms of a contract, it can be found to have waived its right to insist on strict compliance, at least until it gives notice that it will enforce the terms again.

## SUPPORT
Six consecutive acceptances without objection are the kind of consistent pattern that supports a waiver finding; one isolated acceptance would not be enough.

## VERIFICATION:INCONCLUSIVE | explicita
Let me check one thing that could change the analysis: whether the lease contains a non-waiver clause stating that acceptance of late payment does not waive the landlord's rights. Courts treat such clauses in different ways, and the facts given say nothing about one. So I cannot settle this point either way, and I will proceed on the facts as stated.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
On the facts stated, the landlord established a pattern of accepting late payment, so the tenant could reasonably rely on it. To reinstate the strict due date, the landlord needed to notify the tenant that timely payment would again be required. Terminating without that notice is the kind of abrupt enforcement that the waiver doctrine prevents.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the most likely outcome is that the termination is invalid because the landlord waived strict compliance until giving notice, option B.

Thus final answer: B.
