A chest radiograph shows a 4-cm mass adjacent to the right hilum. Serum calcium is 12.4 mg/dL (normal 8.5–10.5) and serum parathyroid hormone is low. Which of the following is the most likely histologic type of this patient's tumor? Options: A. Small cell carcinoma B. Squamous cell carcinoma C. Adenocarcinoma D. Large cell carcinoma
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I gather the facts. A 58-year-old heavy smoker has three months of cough, weight loss, and hemoptysis, and his radiograph shows a 4-cm mass next to the right hilum. His calcium is high at 12.4 mg/dL, and his parathyroid hormone is low.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
A hilar mass in a heavy smoker with hemoptysis and weight loss is a lung cancer arising in a central bronchus, the part of the airway most exposed to cigarette smoke.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The laboratory pattern is the key clue. Calcium is high while parathyroid hormone is suppressed, so the calcium is being driven by something other than the parathyroid glands. In a patient with lung cancer, that something is a paraneoplastic hormone, parathyroid hormone-related peptide.

## SUPPORT
Parathyroid hormone-related peptide acts on the same receptor as parathyroid hormone, so it raises calcium, and the high calcium in turn suppresses the normal parathyroid glands and their hormone.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The lung tumor that secretes parathyroid hormone-related peptide is squamous cell carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma typically arises centrally in the large bronchi of heavy smokers.

## SUPPORT
Central squamous tumors often bleed into the airway as they grow, which accounts for the blood-streaked sputum.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most likely histologic type is squamous cell carcinoma, option B.

Final answer: B.
