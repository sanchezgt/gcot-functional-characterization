## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Okay, let's see. 50 mL of 2.0 M HCl mixed with 500 mL of water. I need the new concentration.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Moles of HCl: 2.0 mol/L x 0.050 L = 0.10 mol. Dilution doesn't change the number of moles, only the volume they're spread over: 50 + 500 = 550 mL.

## VERIFICATION:INCONCLUSIVE | implicita
That 550 mL assumes the two volumes simply add, and the problem says nothing about any volume change on mixing; for a dilute aqueous solution such a change is small, but the data don't pin it down.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Taking the final volume as 550 mL: concentration = 0.10 mol / 0.550 L = 0.18 mol/L.

## SUPPORT
The relation behind this is C1V1 = C2V2: the amount of solute before and after dilution is the same.
