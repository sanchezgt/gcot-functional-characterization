enunciado: A 24-year-old tall, thin man who smokes comes to the emergency department with sudden right-sided chest pain and shortness of breath that began 1 hour ago while he was watching television. He has no history of trauma or recent travel. His pulse is 102/min and oxygen saturation is 94% on room air. Breath sounds are absent over the right hemithorax, which is hyperresonant to percussion. The trachea is midline. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis? Options: A. Pulmonary embolism B. Primary spontaneous pneumothorax C. Community-acquired pneumonia D. Acute pericarditis
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I note the key facts: a young, tall, thin smoker with sudden chest pain and dyspnea at rest, no trauma, no recent travel, mild tachycardia and borderline oxygen saturation. The exam shows absent breath sounds and hyperresonance on the right with a midline trachea.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
Alternatively, this could be a pulmonary embolism, since sudden chest pain with dyspnea and tachycardia fits that diagnosis too. But a clot in the pulmonary circulation does not abolish breath sounds or make one hemithorax hyperresonant. So I set pulmonary embolism aside.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Absent breath sounds with hyperresonance on one side mean air in the pleural space, and with no trauma that is a spontaneous pneumothorax. In a tall, thin young smoker with no underlying lung disease, this is a primary spontaneous pneumothorax, typically from rupture of an apical subpleural bleb.

## SUPPORT
The tall, thin body habitus and smoking are the classic risk factors for rupture of an apical bleb.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the most likely diagnosis is a primary spontaneous pneumothorax, option B.

Final answer: B.
