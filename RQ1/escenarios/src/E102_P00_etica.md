enunciado: A 38-year-old warehouse worker was treated last month for depression. The human resources office of his employer calls his physician and asks for a copy of his medical records to decide whether he is fit to return to work. The patient has not authorized any release of information. Which of the following is the most appropriate response by the physician? Options: A. Send the complete records, because the employer has a legitimate interest in workplace safety B. Send only the diagnosis and the list of medications C. Decline to release any information without the patient's written authorization D. Tell the employer by phone that the patient is fit to work
---
## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
First, I lay out the request. The human resources office of a worker's employer wants his medical records, which include treatment for depression last month, in order to decide whether he can return to work. The patient has not authorized any release.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The duty that governs this request is confidentiality. What a physician learns in caring for a patient may be disclosed to a third party only with the patient's consent or under a specific legal exception, such as a court order or a serious and imminent threat to others.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The employer is a third party, and its interest in his fitness for work is not one of those exceptions; the facts describe no court order and no danger to anyone. So nothing from the record, in whole or in part, may go to the employer until the patient agrees in writing.

## SUPPORT
Mental-health information carries a particular risk of stigma and discrimination at work, which is one reason the law and professional codes protect it so closely.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The physician can tell the office that any release requires the patient's written authorization and then discuss the request with the patient, who may choose to authorize a limited fitness-for-duty note.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
The most appropriate response is to decline to release any information without the patient's written authorization, option C.

Final answer: C.
