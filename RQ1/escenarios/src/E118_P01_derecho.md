## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: an arrested burglary suspect, at the police station and during fingerprinting, says "I did it" without having been asked any question and before any Miranda warnings. The defense moves to suppress.

We need to decide whether the statement must be suppressed.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation, and both elements must be present: custody and interrogation. He had been arrested and was held at the station, so he was in custody.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The remaining element is interrogation, which means express questioning or words or actions by the police that they should know are reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response. No officer asked him anything; he said "I did it" on his own. So the statement was not the product of interrogation.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me check that conclusion against the facts, since fingerprinting is a moment when officers do speak to an arrestee. The facts say that no officer had asked him any question or spoken to him about the burglary, so there was neither express questioning nor any remark about the crime that could work as its functional equivalent. The conclusion holds: there was no interrogation.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Without interrogation, Miranda does not apply, and a volunteered statement is admissible even though no warnings had been given.

## SUPPORT
The Miranda rule targets the compulsion inherent in police questioning of a person in custody; a statement made freely and without questioning involves no such compulsion.
