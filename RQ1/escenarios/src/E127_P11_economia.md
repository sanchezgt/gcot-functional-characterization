## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: "A perfectly competitive firm has the short-run total cost function TC = 100 + 2q + q^2. The market price is $20 per unit. What output level maximizes the firm's profit?"

We need the profit-maximizing output of a price-taking firm.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | explicita
Alternatively, one might pick the output that minimizes average total cost, since that is the efficient scale: ATC = 100/q + 2 + q is lowest at q = 10. But a price taker maximizes profit by comparing price with marginal cost, not by producing at minimum ATC; the two coincide only in long-run equilibrium, when the price has fallen to that minimum. So I set the minimum-ATC output aside.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
Marginal cost is the derivative of total cost: MC = 2 + 2q. Setting price equal to marginal cost, 20 = 2 + 2q, so q = 9.

## SUPPORT
A price-taking firm can sell every additional unit at $20, so each extra unit adds to profit as long as its marginal cost is below $20.

## VERIFICATION:CONFIRMED | explicita
Let me double-check that q = 9 is a maximum and that the firm should produce at all. MC rises with q, so P = MC at q = 9 is a maximum, not a minimum. At q = 9, revenue is 180 and total cost is 100 + 18 + 81 = 199, a loss of 19; shutting down would lose the whole fixed cost of 100. Average variable cost at q = 9 is 2 + 9 = 11, below the price of 20. So producing 9 units is confirmed as the best choice.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
Thus final answer: q = 9.
