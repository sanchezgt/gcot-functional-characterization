## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
We are given: "A 6-year-old boy is brought to the physician because of a sore throat and fever for 2 days. He has no cough or rhinorrhea. His temperature is 38.9°C. Examination shows erythematous, swollen tonsils with white exudates and tender, enlarged anterior cervical lymph nodes. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?"

We need the next step in the evaluation of this pharyngitis.

## ALTERNATIVE:REJECTED | implicita
Infectious mononucleosis can produce exudative tonsillitis with fever, but it is a disease of adolescents and young adults with posterior cervical and often generalized lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, and marked fatigue, whereas this patient is 6 years old with tender anterior nodes only.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
The combination of fever, tonsillar exudates, tender anterior cervical nodes, and the absence of cough is the classic picture of group A streptococcal pharyngitis in a school-age child. These four features are the Centor criteria, and this patient has all of them.

## PROGRESSION:ADVANCES
When the pretest probability of streptococcal pharyngitis is high, the next step is to confirm it with a rapid streptococcal antigen test, because antibiotics are indicated only for confirmed streptococcal infection, to prevent rheumatic fever and shorten the illness.

## SUPPORT
In children a negative rapid test is followed by a throat culture, because the rapid test can miss some cases.

## PROGRESSION:REACHES_OUTPUT
So the most appropriate next step is the rapid streptococcal antigen test, option A.

Thus final answer: A.
